# T-NEW — The thirsty go out under the alert

**Source:** [T-653's probe](../../reports/data/2026-11-20-t653-ambush-at-the-all-clear.md) · **Size:** M · **Needs:** — · **Status:** open

## Why

In both springs T-653 traced, most of the dead were not caught at work: they had left the shelter for drink. In `three-years-p4-1` at shift 100 the fortress held 20 drinks for 42 dwarves on day 280; after four days behind the gate for a raid (days 292 to 295), dozens were at critical thirst, and the moment the gate's doors opened, for the all-clear or for T-653's look alike, they came up the stair together for the drink by the gate's south door and for the brook, through the ambush (31 dead on master; 22 in a replay where the look made it spring on the squad instead, the crowd coming out regardless). In `three-years-p4-4` at shift 150, on day 261, twelve dwarves were outside the gate when a wolf alert locked it, most of them at the brook for drink or asleep on the ground beside it, and an ambush sprang on them (13 dead). A critical need overrides the shelter (10 § Behavior selection, step 2), as it should; what a player would not allow is a fortress that cannot drink below ground. Opening the gate for the dwarves locked out, tried in T-653's replay of `p4-4`, changed nothing (13 dead), so it was not kept.

## What to do

Find, over a sweep's peril-4 timelines, how often an alert ends with dwarves at critical thirst, and where they go to drink. Then answer as a player would, in the script: keep the drink below paced to the population (the brewer's orders and plants against the dwarves' count, and a reserve that lasts a raid's alert of four days), and keep no drink where the thirsty must cross the gate to reach it. If brewing cannot keep up at year-3 populations, say so with the figures, and consider a well over water below ground where the map has it.

Spec: testing-strategy.md § The long scenarios (the drink and the cave farm).

## Done

The thirst at the end of each alert, and the dwarves outside under it, before and after, by run; the sweep's verdict.
