# 03 Terrain & Mining

**Owner:** `systems/terrain` · **Depends on:** [`../01-architecture/data-model.md`](../01-architecture/data-model.md), [`../01-architecture/constants.md`](../01-architecture/constants.md), [01-world-generation.md](01-world-generation.md), [11-jobs-and-labor.md](11-jobs-and-labor.md)

## Purpose

Generate the local map from a `LocalMapSpec`, and keep `Map3D` correct as the fortress digs, builds, burns and floods. Own designations and the jobs that carry them out. Own discovery.

## Concepts

- **Column**: all tiles at one `(x, y)`. The **surface** of a column is its highest non-open tile.
- **Layer**: a horizontal band of rock of one material. Layers are generated as a stack per column with small undulation.
- **Vein**: a random-walk path of ore tiles through a host layer. **Cluster**: a blob of gem or ore tiles.
- **Cavern**: a natural open region within a cavern band, with a floor, pillars, lakes and flora.
- **Designation**: a player mark on a tile that becomes a job.
- **Support**: a construction's requirement to touch a solid tile.
- **Discovery**: whether the player can see a tile.

## Rules

### Local map generation

Stages, each with its own rng stream (`terrain.<stage>`), run in order:

1. **Heightmap.** Base surface z from region elevation: `surfaceZ = SURFACE_Z_MIN + (elevation − SEA_LEVEL) × (SURFACE_Z_MAX − SURFACE_Z_MIN) / (255 − SEA_LEVEL)`. Relief 0 is level ground: every column stands at the base. From relief 1, add two octaves of noise (periods 32 and 12, weights 0.7 and 0.3) of amplitude `amp = [0, 3, 5, 8][relief]`, less `amp / 2`, so even relief 1 swells a level up and down. Round noise offsets to the nearer level, with exact half-level ties toward the base. Add ridges: a third field (one octave, period 40) rises where it lies within a fifth of its middle, linearly to `[0, 1, 2, 3][relief]` levels at the middle, so ridges run along its middle contour. Clamp to `SURFACE_Z_MIN..SURFACE_Z_MAX`. Smooth with one pass of a 3×3 median so cliffs are rare except in high relief. Then, from relief 1, on their own stream (`terrain.relief`), raise outcrops and crags, both bare rock with no soil (stage 3): `outcrops + 3 × (relief − 1)` outcrops (the biome's count, per 96 × 96 tiles, in `regions-biomes-and-traits.md` § Biome surface), each a disc of radius 1–2 (1–3 at relief 3) centred at least 3 tiles from the edge, raised one level above its centre's surface (one or two at relief 3); where the biome has crags, `crags + 2 × (relief − 1)` crags, each a cone of radius 2–4 whose peak stands 2–4 levels plus `relief − 1` above its centre, falling linearly to one level at its rim, with a ring of scree two columns wide around it (stage 9). Raising never lowers a column.
2. **Rivers and lakes.** For a river, trace a path from the midpoint of each inflow edge to the midpoint of the outflow edge (or to a lake center if no outflow) with a wandering random walk biased toward the target; widen to the class width (brook 1, river 3, major 5). All the map's water stands at one level, held below its *water surface*: the lowest ground among the dry columns cardinally beside the river's bands and the lake's disc, before any carving. Each width band is carved to the water surface minus the class depth (brook 1, river 2, major 2), whatever the ground above it, so a river through high ground runs in a cut; the bed is a stone `floor`, and level-7 water fills the bed tile through one level below the water surface. Every bank is therefore `wall` at the water's top, at least one bank stands one level above the water, where it can be drunk from (10), and the water has no step for a source to drive it over a bank (04). Where a river runs in a cut it has *drinking steps*: at every `DRINK_STEP_SPACING` tiles of its path, on both banks and straight across the way it runs, a lane one column wide whose floor is one level above the water at the bank and rises one level a column until it meets the ground. A lane that would leave the map or meet water is not cut. A lake has one such lane from its centre in each cardinal direction. From relief 1 a lane's columns grow no tree or shrub and hold no boulder. The inflow tiles at the edge are marked `source`; the outflow tiles are marked `sink` (04). A river with no inflow edge (the region where it rises) carves one path from a spring: a point drawn by the stream at least `SPRING_EDGE_MARGIN` tiles from every edge and, when there is an outflow edge, in the half of the map away from it; the spring's width band is marked `source` in place of inflow tiles. A lake has a centre chosen uniformly where the largest radius fits and in the half of the map on the arrival edge's side, so its water lies within the drink search (10) of a dwarf at the wagon, radius uniform 6–12, and a plain disc edge without jitter. Its bed is two below the water surface, filled from the bed tile through one below the water surface, with no sources.
3. **Soil.** The top `soilDepth` tiles of each column (biome-dependent, 1–5; 0 on `glasslands` and `alpine`) are of a soil material chosen per tile by the biome's weights (loam, clay, sand, peat, silt): the top one is the column's surface `floor`, and the `soilDepth − 1` below it are `wall`. A site one level deep (`tundra`, `mountain`, `desert`, `badlands`) therefore has its soil only in its surface floors, which a `channel` digs out (§ Tile transitions). Below the surface in a river bed the soil is replaced by the stone below. Outcrop and crag columns have no soil: their surface is rock (on `glasslands`, obsidian as everywhere).
4. **Rock layers.** Per geology class, a layer template lists candidate materials in order from top to bottom with thickness ranges (content). The stack is generated once per map with thicknesses rolled from the ranges, then applied to every column with an undulation of ±1 z from low-frequency noise, so layers tilt gently. The stack is anchored at the surface and extends to z = `MAGMA_SEA_TOP_Z + 1`; the stack repeats if it runs out.
5. **Sea of Fire.** z in `0..MAGMA_SEA_TOP_Z`: all tiles `open` with magma level 7 and `source`, except basalt pillars (noise threshold 8%) and the outer 2-tile rim which is basalt wall. z = `MAGMA_SEA_TOP_Z + 1` is a wall layer of basalt (the sea's roof).
6. **Caverns.** For each band, a 3D noise field whose lowest-noise `CAVERN_OPEN_FRACTION` of interior tiles is opened (count rounded down, ties by tile position; the two-tile rim stays closed) (scaled by `deepness`: 0.25 at 0 to 0.55 at 255; `fungal_depths` adds 0.15; `deep_well` makes cavern one 0.7 and mostly water). Open tiles in the band become `open`; the tile below the lowest open tile in each column becomes `floor`. Cavern lakes select the lowest-noise 18% of floor tiles (60% in cavern one with `deep_well`), with ties by position and counts rounded down; water fills from the floor through one above the band's lowest open level, stopping at the band ceiling or a blocking tile. Each dry floor tile in cavern two and three rolls a 2‰ chance for a magma pool (level 7, no source, radius 2–4). Pillars are left where noise is highest. Cavern floors get fungal flora (07) and the band's species list (08). Caverns do not connect to the surface or to each other except through the noise; connections are not forced.

    **The bat roosts** follow stage 6, on their own stream (`terrain.roosts`), so no other stream draws differently and the map's tiles are as they were: each floor tile of cavern one and two (`cavernFloors`) with an `open` tile above it and a natural `wall` above that (the cavern's ceiling over open floor) becomes a roost's drop tile with `ROOST_PERMILLE` (doubled by `fungal_depths`), drawn in ascending tile order, cavern one first. The drop tiles are recorded, ascending, in `LocalMapResult.roosts` for 08, which keeps them (08 § Bat roosts). Nothing is drawn: a roost is not a tile kind, and the local map's hash does not include it.
7. **Veins and clusters.** For each mineral row in the content table, the expected count per map is `base × wealthMultiplier[wealth]` with `ironheart` and `gemsprings` adding to specific minerals. Veins: choose a random start tile in a host layer (the mineral lists host materials), random-walk `length` steps with 6-direction moves biased horizontally, painting a 1–2 tile radius; clusters: a blob of radius 1–3. Deepsilver: only at `z ≤ DEEPSILVER_Z_MAX`, only in walls adjacent (within 2) to the Sea of Fire rim or to a magma pool, count by wealth (0 at wealth 0–1). `wyrm_roost` guarantees one deepsilver vein next to the wyrm's chamber in cavern three. Ores, gems and deepsilver draw on the stage's stream in that order; a mineral row added after version 1 (a row of `row-order.md`: `halite`, `bog_iron`, `giant_bone`) is placed after the deepsilver, each on its own stream (`terrain.minerals.<id>`), so the older minerals of a map lie where they did. A row of count 0 (`star_iron`) is placed by no stage-7 draw: the `starfall` crater places it (stage 10). `giant_bone` is a mineral of class `bone` (a fossil), placed in the sedimentary layers as any mineral is.
8. **Volcano.** If `volcano`: a magma pipe of radius 1–2 from the Sea of Fire to the surface at a random location at least 20 tiles from the arrival edge; the surface around it for radius 6 is obsidian and bare. On `glasslands` the whole surface soil layer is replaced by obsidian and the pipe radius is 3, with two more shallow magma pools at cavern one depth.
9. **Slopes, boulders, trees and vegetation.** First the slopes: every dry surface `floor` one level below a cardinal neighbour's dry surface `floor` or `ramp` (the neighbour's tile on its level is `wall`) becomes a `ramp` of its own material, so every one-level step of the surface can be walked, up a slope of several steps as up one (the neighbour may itself be a ramp); two levels or more stay a cliff. Then, from relief 1, loose boulders (stream `terrain.boulders`): each dry surface `floor` outside the volcano's bare ground and the drinking steps becomes a `boulder` with the biome's `boulders` probability (`regions-biomes-and-traits.md` § Biome surface), three times that on bare rock and at least 250‰ in a crag's scree; a boulder is of the first rock under its column's soil. Then, on soil surface tiles, place trees by the biome's tree density and species weights (trees are `tree` kind at the surface tile; trees occupy one tile and are 1 tile tall in version 1); shrubs by shrub density; grass flag on remaining soil floors. Trees are not placed within 2 tiles of the river bed except willows. Cave shrubs follow on cavern floors, and then, on their own stream (`terrain.rare_patches`), each cave shrub may turn into a rare species with its `rarePermille` by cavern tier (`plants.md` § Rare cave patches). Only a tile that is already a cave shrub changes, and the other streams draw the same numbers. Last, lying snow, where the biome has a `snow` share: a dry surface `floor` or `ramp` carries `snow` where a patch field (two octaves, periods 14 and 5, weights 0.7 and 0.3, from stream `terrain.snow`), raised a tenth for each level its surface stands above the base surface, lies in the top `snow` of its range; 06 treats it as snow cover. The surface keeps its kind of resources: soil, trees, shrubs and grass only where the biome has them.

    **The boneyards** (stage 9b) follow, after stage 9 and before stage 10, on their own stream (`terrain.giant_bones`): a map whose biome is `desert`, `badlands`, `wetland` or `tundra` lays `BONEYARD_MIN`–`BONEYARD_MAX` skeletons of `giant_bone`, the bones of something enormous. Every other biome draws nothing from the stream, so its map is unchanged; the stages after it draw on the four biomes' ground as the skeletons left it. A map narrower or shorter than twice `BONEYARD_EDGE_MARGIN` (a test fixture) lays none. Each skeleton is tried from up to `BONEYARD_TRIES` starts; each try draws, in order, the start's column (`x`, then `y`, each uniform from `BONEYARD_EDGE_MARGIN` to the far side less it), the spine's length (`BONEYARD_SPINE_MIN`–`BONEYARD_SPINE_MAX`), its heading (one of the four cardinals), whether it bends (500‰), after which column (from the fourth to its fifth from last) and which way (500‰ clockwise), so a try that fails draws what one that fits does. A start fits when it lies at least `BONEYARD_EDGE_MARGIN` inward of the arrival edge's wagon search band (02 § Arrival: `ARRIVAL_SEARCH_DEPTH` + `BONEYARD_EDGE_MARGIN` from that edge), its surface tile is a dry `floor` that is not a drinking step or volcanic glass, no column within `BONEYARD_CLEARANCE` holds water or another skeleton, and no tree stands beside it (only beside it: a wooded wetland has a tree within three tiles of nearly every column). The shape: a spine from the start, a straight or once-bent line of columns, turning a quarter after the drawn column when it bends; a pair of ribs, one column out on each side, at every second spine column from the third to the last but one; the cage, the columns beside the spine between its ribs; and a skull, a 2×2 just beyond the spine's first column, on the spine's line and the column clockwise of it, whose two columns farthest from the spine are its tusks. Where two parts claim a column the skull wins, then the spine, then a rib. The shape fits when every column of it lies at least two from every edge, outside the wagon search band, and more than `BONEYARD_CLEARANCE` from every column of another skeleton; otherwise the try fails. A fitting shape is laid on each column's surface tile: the spine's tiles become `boulder`s of `giant_bone`, the ribs' and the skull's `wall`s of `giant_bone` one level tall in place of the surface tile (where the surface is soil, the soil floor becomes the bone wall, so the skeleton lies half sunk), their `grass` and `snow` cleared. A column whose surface tile is not a dry `floor`, stands more than one level from the start's, or is a drinking step is left as it was: the skeleton is laid with a gap. In `wetland` a rib whose surface `floor` holds water no deeper than `BONEYARD_RIB_WATER_MAX` becomes a wall all the same, standing in the bog. In `tundra` the skull's two tusk tiles are `ivory` walls in place of bone: dug, each drops a `tusk`. The skull's laid tiles are recorded as one group (`giantSkulls`, ascending); the cage's walkable tiles (a floor, ramp, shrub or sapling with no fluid) are recorded per skeleton (`boneyards`, ascending), even for a skeleton whose cage is empty, so a skeleton's index is its place in the list. The site summary (02) already lists giant bones for these biomes as likely, not certain (`regions-biomes-and-traits.md` § Signatures).
10. **Trait features.** `old_battlefield`: scatter `BATTLEFIELD_ITEM_COUNT` rusted iron weapons and armor, choosing uniformly among the metal-made rows of those tables, at quality 0; scatter the bone piles and skulls of the region trait as bone. All lie on dry surface floors 15–40 tiles from the arrival edge. After the other traits, on its own stream (`terrain.battlefield_channel`), an `old_battlefield` map holds a dead runecaster's channel with `BATTLEFIELD_CHANNEL_PERMILLE`: one row drawn uniformly among the `weapons` rows with a launcher and no ammunition, at quality 0, on a tile of the same band. A metal one lies in iron with no stone; a wooden one is of a material of class `wood` drawn uniformly and keeps a stone drawn uniformly among the `stone`s of the `spells` table ([25-runecraft.md](25-runecraft.md) § Channels). There is at most one, the trait's other picks draw the same numbers, and no other map holds one. `sunken_halls`: choose the highest dry surface floor in the far half of the map where the footprint fits (every column from one west of the entrance to 16 east of it, and 4 rows each side, is dry and stands above the hall level, so the hall lies in rock); among equal heights prefer greater inward distance from the arrival edge, then column order. Two levels below that entrance, carve an 11-tile corridor east, six 3×3 rooms (three on each side, each with a one-tile opening to the corridor), and a stairwell at the corridor’s end down two further levels. A 5×5 vault begins two tiles east of the foot of the stair, sealed by the intervening rock. A stair shaft joins the surface entrance to the corridor. Spawn 12 common furniture items in the rooms’ own stone, drawn from `bed`, `table`, `chair`, `chest`, `cabinet`, `door`; the vault holds `VAULT_TREASURE` deepsilver items (axe, helm, breastplate) and a sleeping `blind_stalker`. `gemsprings`: choose three dry surface-soil patches of 2×2 tiles nearest a river tile drawn by the stream, or nearest the map centre when there is no river; keep the patches one tile apart. Their tiles are `open` with water level 7, recorded as warm springs for 05 so they never freeze. `wyrm_roost`: carve a 9×9 floor at the bottom of cavern three with two levels of headroom; start its deepsilver vein in the wall immediately east when that wall meets the deepsilver magma-distance rule; record the sleeping wyrm. Item and creature entries are recorded in `LocalMapResult.spawns` for arrival.

    **The conversions** follow every other trait feature, so no other stage reads what they place: they place the wild plants no biome weight places (`plants.md` § Conversions): one pass per conversion row, in the `plants` table's order, each on its own stream (`terrain.convert.<plant id>`), and only for a region its row names (or every region, for a row marked `any`). Each pass takes every column's surface tile, in column order (row by row, `y` then `x`), that is eligible by the row (an existing shrub, an existing tree, a grass floor, a dry sand or soil floor, a bare rock floor, a floor of soil or a shrub within `CONVERT_WATER_RADIUS` columns of a water column, a floor of soil in water no deeper than `CONVERT_SHALLOW_MAX`, or a floor of `ash_soil`), and turns it with the row's permille. A turned floor becomes a `shrub` of the plant and loses its `grass` and `snow`; a turned tree keeps its tile and changes only its species (`ironwood`). A tile one pass turned is not eligible for another. The main stage-9 stream draws the same numbers, so the rock, the trees and the shrubs of a map are those it had, and only a turned tile changes; a region no row names draws nothing. The site summary (02) lists the converted plants with the trees and the shrubs.

    **The crater** of `starfall` ends stage 10, after the conversions, on its own stream (`terrain.starfall`), so a map without the trait draws nothing and every other stream of a map with it draws what it would without it: only the crater's ground changes. Its radius is drawn uniformly in `CRATER_RADIUS_MIN`–`CRATER_RADIUS_MAX`. Its centre is drawn uniformly among the columns, in column order, at least `CRATER_RADIUS_MAX + 2` from every edge and `CRATER_EDGE_DISTANCE` from the arrival edge, whose surface tile is a dry `floor`, where every column within the radius plus 2 is dry with no fluid from two levels below the centre's surface up to its own, and every column within the radius stands no lower than one level below the centre, with natural ground on top (a floor, ramp, tree, sapling, shrub or boulder) and `wall` from two levels below the centre up to it, so the crater is only ever dug and cuts open no hall, spring or cavern. Where no column fits, the radius is one less, down to `CRATER_RADIUS_MIN`; a map where none fits even then has no crater. Every column within the radius (`dx² + dy² ≤ r²`) is lowered so that its floor stands one level below the centre's surface: every tile above is opened (rock, soil, trees, shrubs and boulders go), and the floor tile is a `floor` of `ash_soil`, ash for good (07: ash on `ash_soil` never clears), its flags cleared. A ring of ramps joins the crater to the ground beside it where the step is one level: a crater tile beside a column one level higher becomes a `ramp`, and a dry `floor` beside the crater one level lower becomes one. Then each crater `floor`, in column order, turns into a shrub of `wild_wyrmtongue` with `CRATER_WYRMTONGUE_PERMILLE` on the same stream. One level below the centre's floor, in a pocket opened for it, lies a `boulder` of `star_iron`, the only one on the map (`star_iron` has a mineral count of 0, so stage 7 places none); dug, it drops one `ore`, which the smelter makes into three bars of star steel. The crater's tiles at its floor's level (floors, ramps and wyrmtongue) are recorded, ascending (`craterTiles`, saved by 03); an item or creature of the spawn list above a crater column's floor lies on that floor.
    **The ruins** (stage 10b) of the fortresses that ended on the site ([02-embark-and-loadout.md](02-embark-and-loadout.md) § Ruins) are laid last, in the order the fortresses ended, drawing nothing, so a site with none is unchanged: each ruin tile takes its record's kind and material and its `constructed` and `smoothed` flags, loses its engraving, `grass` and `snow`, and a `wall` or `fortification` loses its fluid. Then the last ruin's items join the spawn list (`source: 'ruins'`, with their `count` and quality), each where its tile is still solid-bottomed (a floor, ramp, stairs, sapling or shrub); one whose tile has fallen, or whose type or material the content no longer has, is left out. The last ruin's standing furniture (T-690) goes to arrival with the map (`LocalMapResult.ruinFurniture`), each piece whose building type, item and material the content still has; 14 builds it there where its tiles still stand (02 § Ruins). So do its wrecks and armed traps (`LocalMapResult.ruinWrecks`), each whose building type and material the content still has, with the units and loaded items whose item type and material it still has (an armed trap left with no loaded item is a wreck); 14 places them as wrecks, and an armed trap built and armed (14 § Wrecks).
11. **Discovery and lighting.** All outdoor tiles and the tiles adjacent to them are `discovered`; `outdoor` and `lit` flags computed per column.
12. **Connectivity.** Ask 22 to build initial connectivity labels.

The generator runs synchronously with progress callbacks after each stage; budget 1.5 s.

### Tile transitions (what designations do)

| Designation | Precondition | Result | Item produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dig` | `wall` or `boulder`, not `constructed` | `floor` with the same material; the tile above is unchanged | Per material class: `stone` and `obsidian`: `raw_stone` with `MINING_STONE_DROP_PERMILLE`; `ore`: always `ore`; `gem`: always `rough_gem`; `bone`: always a `fossil` (a `tusk` for `ivory`, a tundra boneyard's tusks); `soil` whose material carries the tag `soil_drop` (`clay`, `sand`, `peat`): a `soil_load` with `SOIL_DROP_PERMILLE`; every other class, and every other soil: none |
| `channel` | `floor`, `ramp`, `sapling`, `shrub`, or `stairs_down`, that holds no building | Tile becomes `open`; if the tile below is a `wall` that is not `constructed`, it becomes `ramp`; if it is a `constructed` wall or a `floor` it stays; if it is `open` nothing more | A `floor` of a soil tagged `soil_drop`, not `constructed`, drops as if dug: its soil is dug out. Then drops from the tile below as if dug when it becomes a ramp |
| `stairs_up` | `wall`, not `constructed`, or a `floor` that holds no building | `stairs_up` | As dig from a `wall`; none from a `floor` |
| `stairs_down` | a `floor` that holds no building | `stairs_down` | None |
| `stairs_updown` | `wall`, not `constructed`, or a `floor` that holds no building, or a `stairs_up` or `stairs_down` that is not `constructed` (re-carved to lead both ways) | `stairs_updown` | As dig from a `wall`; none otherwise |
| `ramp` | `wall`, not `constructed`, with an `open` tile above, or a `floor` that holds no building | `ramp`; the tile above becomes `open` if it was `floor` | As dig |
| `smooth` | natural `wall` or `floor` of stone | sets `smoothed` | None |
| `engrave` | `smoothed` tile | sets `engraved`; quality from the economy `rollQuality(engraving skill)` using 03's stream, with motif 0, is stored in a side table | None |
| `remove` | `constructed` tile | the construction's material returns as an item; `wall` or `floor` becomes `open`, except a constructed floor over natural rock becomes the natural floor; stairs and ramps become `open` | The block, log or bar used |
| `chop` | `tree` or `sapling` | `floor` (grass if soil); logs by species size for a tree | A tree: 1–4 logs; a sapling: none |
| `gather` | `shrub` | shrub stays; regrowth timer set (07) | The shrub's product, and its second product if its row has one (09 § Gathering) |
| `harvest_ice` | frozen outdoor water: an ice `wall` or ice `floor` of 04's frozen record, on an `outdoor` tile; not cut in the last `ICE_RECUT_DAYS` | the tile stays as it was: ice is cut, not dug out, and the water under it stays | One `stone_block` of `ice`, on the worker's tile |
| `ignite` | a tile 05 calls burnable (`materials.burnableTile`), not `wet`, not `snow`, not `burning` | none here: `TileIgnited` and 05 light it | None (a `firebrand` is spent) |

Every drop is of the dug tile's material. When a terrain job leaves the last tile of a boneyard's skull group (`giantSkulls`) no longer a `wall`, a `giant_skull` of `giant_bone` drops beside that tile's fossil, once: the group leaves the list. The soil drop's roll is on its own stream, `rngFor('03.soil_drop')`, drawn only for a tile of such a soil, so digging stone, ore and every other soil draws the same numbers as before. A channel through a soil floor over a soil wall rolls twice, the floor first, as two tiles of soil come out.

A `constructed` wall is never carved: `dig`, stairs and `ramp` refuse it, and a channel above it leaves it standing. It comes down only by `remove`, which returns what it was built of.

Digging a `wall` that has a `floor` above it (which is every underground wall) never affects the tile above. Digging removes the last support of nothing, because natural rock is never subject to support.

A removed or collapsed construction returns 14's recorded input item type. When no record exists, its material class supplies a fallback: wood returns a `log`, metal a `bar`, anything else a `stone_block`. Drops are also recorded in `terrain.intendedDrops()`, and sent to the item spawn API when present.

When a designation is applied to a tile that no longer matches its precondition (because something else changed it), the designation is cleared silently, except a `harvest_ice` designation on thawed water, which waits for the ice (§ Ice harvesting). A designation worked standing on its own tile (channel, down stairs, and smoothing or engraving a floor) is cleared in the same way, with its job, when a building other than a door that blocks walking on the tile is built there (14 `BuildingCompleted`), and is cleared when it is made on such a tile, before any job: nobody can stand there to do the work.

Nothing is cut under a building: no stairs, no channel and no ramp. A `floor` that holds a building of any kind, planned or built (furniture, a workshop, a door, a construction, the wagon), fails the precondition of every stair designation; a tile that holds one (a floor, or a hatch's down stairs) fails `channel`, which would open it; and a `wall` under a floor that holds one fails `ramp`, which would open that floor. `Designate` refuses such a tile and the tools skip it (24 § Input model). A building placed on a tile that holds a stair or channel designation clears the designation, and one placed on the floor above a wall designated for a ramp clears the ramp's, each with its job, when it is placed (14 `BuildingPlaced`); a stair, channel or ramp job that completes under a building placed since finds its precondition failed and carves nothing. A natural stair re-carved to up/down stairs is not a floor, so a hatch over it does not stop that. Nothing else opens a tile under a building that is not a construction: natural tiles never collapse (§ Support and collapse), fluids change no tile's kind, and a construction that collapses, melts or burns holds no other building on its tile.

### Digging as a job

- A designation creates a job of type `dig`, `channel`, `carve_stairs`, `carve_ramp`, `smooth`, `engrave`, `remove_construction`, `chop`, `gather`, `harvest_ice` or `ignite` with the designation's priority, through the `terrain` job producer.
- `dig`, `channel`, carving and `remove_construction` require the `mining` labor and a pick in hand (the worker fetches one from a stockpile if not carrying one; picks are tools that a miner keeps until the labor is disabled). `chop` requires `woodcutting` and an axe. `smooth` and `engrave` require `masonry` (smoothing) and `engraving` (engraving) labors and no tool. `gather` requires `herbalism` and no tool.
- Work time: `baseTicks × hardness(material) / (100 + SKILL_TIME_BONUS_PERCENT × skillLevel)`, then the mood multiplier (15). Progress is stored on the job and survives interruption.
- The worker stands on the target for channel, down stairs, or floor smoothing and engraving; other jobs use an adjacent walkable tile in the same-level 8-neighborhood. Up and up/down stairs also allow the tile directly above when it is a down or up/down stair, supplied by the producer's `extraStandTiles`. With no valid stand tile, the job is `blocked: unreachable`.
- Channeling: before removing the floor, move the worker to the first passable 8-neighbour in tile order. With none, the movement helper makes it fall after the floor opens (over rock, onto the ramp the channel left below). Every other creature standing on the tile as it opens falls too.
- On completion: apply the transition, spawn the item on the tile, or on the first solid-bottomed tile below when it is now `open`, grant skill experience, emit `TilesChanged` and, for ore or gem dug out of rock (stairs cut into a floor or re-carved from stairs mine nothing), `MineralMined { tile, material }` and on the first tile of a vein `VeinFound { material }`. A tile of class `bone` (a fossil, or a boulder of bone) emits `MineralMined` as an ore does, and no `VeinFound`; the first one dug in a fortress emits `FossilFound { dwarfId, tile }` once (saved flag `fossilFound`): the note "{dwarf} has found the bones of something old." and the digger's thought `first_fossil` (15). Digging into an open cavern tile emits `CavernBreached { cavern }` the first time for each cavern.
- A vein is the face-connected set of tiles of one mineral material, searched with a 4,096-tile bound and keyed by its lowest tile index. Dug tiles retain material, so digging preserves the identity. Only the first mined tile whose key is absent from `veinsFound` emits `VeinFound`.
- A player `CancelJob` clears its designation so it cannot recreate the job. Removing or changing a designation cancels its job with `target_invalid`.
- Deepsilver: each deepsilver tile dug emits `DeepsilverMined { tile }` (18 raises Deep pressure). Before the first deepsilver designation is applied, the UI shows a one-time confirmation (24) explaining the consequence.

### Ice harvesting

- Designation `harvest_ice` on a tile of frozen outdoor water (04's frozen record: an ice `wall` or ice `floor` whose fluid byte holds water with the `frozen` bit, on an `outdoor` tile). The job `harvest_ice` (labor `mining`, skill `mining`, a pick) is worked from a walkable tile beside it on its level, standing on it when it is an ice floor, or from above: the tile directly above it and that tile's eight neighbours, the bank a miner reaches down from (the producer's `extraStandTiles`; an open tile is no stand, so the middle of a frozen lake whose ice is all walls is out of reach until a shore of ice floor leads to it).
- Work time `ICE_HARVEST_TICKS × 100 / (100 + SKILL_TIME_BONUS_PERCENT × skillLevel)`, as digging, with no hardness factor.
- On completion it drops one `stone_block` of `ice` on the worker's tile and leaves the tile as it was: ice is cut, not dug out, and the water under it stays. The tile is recorded in `iceCut` with the tick, and cannot be designated again for `ICE_RECUT_DAYS`: `Designate` leaves such a tile out of its batch, and refuses with `precondition_failed` when no tile is left (an entry whose wait is over is dropped when it is next looked at). No rng.
- A designation outlives a thaw. Outdoor water in a temperate winter freezes by night and thaws by day, and a cut waits for the ice: while its tile is thawed water (an `open` or `floor` tile whose fluid byte holds water, on an `outdoor` tile) the designation stays, has no job (a job already made is cancelled, and ice that thaws under the pick completes nothing) and shows as `blocked` (`terrain.designationWork`); when the tile freezes again its job is made again. It is cleared only when its tile stops being outdoor water: filled or built over, or found drained dry at 03's next scan of its designations. A thawed tile takes no new `harvest_ice` designation.
- An ice block is a `stone_block` of `ice`, so the construction rows take it with no new row: 05 cools and melts what is built of it (05 § Ice constructions), and 13 melts the block itself (13 § Rot).

### Setting a fire

- Designation `ignite` on a tile 05 calls burnable (`materials.burnableTile`: a constructed tile of wood anywhere; outdoors a tree, sapling or shrub, a grass floor, or a floor or wall of a burnable soil), not `wet`, not `snow` and not `burning`. 03's precondition applies the same rule to the map and the material rows, so the designate tool can filter a box without 05.
- The job `ignite` (labor `woodcutting`, no skill, xp 0) fetches one `firebrand` as its input, walks to a tile beside the target, works `IGNITE_TICKS`, spends the firebrand and emits `TileIgnited { tile, dwarfId }`; 05 queues the ignition as it queues a lightning strike's (05 § Fire on purpose). The dwarf gets `set_a_fire` (15). Woodcutting is the labor because clearing land is a woodcutter's work, and children never light fires. A tile that stopped being burnable before the work is done clears its designation, and the firebrand is not spent.
- The first `ignite` designation of a fortress asks for the confirmation `c_first_ignite` (24), as the first deepsilver designation does: `Designate` with kind `ignite` is refused with `ignite_unconfirmed` while the fortress's `igniteConfirmed` is false, and `ConfirmIgnite` (no payload) sets it.

### Support and collapse

- A `constructed` `wall`, `floor`, `ramp`, `stairs_*` or `fortification` is *supported* if any of its six face-adjacent tiles is `wall`, `floor`, `ramp`, `stairs_*`, `fortification` or `tree` (natural or constructed). The tile below counting as support means a floor built on rock is supported by the rock.
- When a tile changes, every constructed neighbor is re-checked. An unsupported construction collapses: it becomes `open` (an unsupported construction has no solid tile below by definition), its material item is spawned at the first solid tile below, creatures on it fall (22 movement helper) and items on it fall. Each terrain action settles its queued support checks until stable in `(z ascending, y, x)` order, then emits one `ConstructionCollapsed` batch if anything collapsed. Several settling actions in one tick can emit several batches. Constructions that touch can support each other; no path to natural ground is required.
- Natural tiles never collapse. Removing all rock around a natural pillar leaves a floating pillar; this is accepted.

### Discovery

- `discovered` is set for: all `outdoor` tiles and their 26 neighbors at generation; any tile changed by a designation and its 26 neighbors; any tile a fortress creature stands on and its 26 neighbors (updated by the movement helper when a creature enters a tile whose neighbors are not all discovered); on `CavernBreached`, the entire connected open region of the cavern reachable from the breach tile within the cavern band (flood fill bounded to the band), plus one tile of rock around it.
- Undiscovered tiles are drawn as blank and are not inspectable. Designations can be placed on undiscovered tiles (the player can plan blind), but the material is shown as "unknown rock".

### Outdoor and lit

- `outdoor` for tile `i` at `(x, y, z)`: every tile above it in the column is `open`. Recomputed for the whole column when any tile in the column changes kind, or when a tile in it turns to or from glass (its material changes to or from one of class `glass`, or a glass tile becomes `constructed`).
- Glass: a constructed `wall` or `floor` whose material is of class `glass` (the material `glass`, 14 § Constructions) passes daylight. Natural `obsidian` is class `obsidian` and passes nothing.
- `skylit` (flag bit 14): a tile that is not `outdoor` and whose every tile above in the column is `open` or a constructed glass floor, with at least one glass floor among them. It is computed with `outdoor`, in the same column pass: scanning down from the sky, a glass floor keeps the light going as an open tile does, and the first other non-open tile is the last tile reached, as for `outdoor` (it is `skylit` too). So any glass floor over open space is a skylight; there is no skylight building. A tile that is `skylit` is `lit`. Light does not spread sideways from a skylit tile. 05 reads `skylit` (a skylit tile is covered), 07 reads it (§ Under a skylight).
- `lit`: `outdoor`, `skylit`, or an `open` or walkable tile that is within `LIGHT_SPREAD` tiles horizontally of an outdoor open tile on the same level with no wall between; a constructed glass wall does not stop the spread, so daylight reaches `LIGHT_SPREAD` tiles through it into the hall behind (the wall tile itself is not `lit`). Recomputed per chunk when the chunk changes. Surface crops need `lit`; cave crops need not `lit`.
- The light flags are flags of the map's `flags` grid and are saved with it; nothing else of the light is saved.

### Restricted traffic

`SetTraffic { tiles, mode: normal | restricted | forbidden }` sets the `restricted` flag (forbidden is restricted plus a stored per-tile mark in the terrain state making it impassable for dwarves; 22 reads both).

### Fluids and constructions interplay

- 04 calls `terrain.setKind` for obsidian, ice and thaw.
- 14 calls `terrain.setKind` for constructions and `terrain.setFlag(constructed)`.
- 05 calls `terrain.setKind` when a burning tree or wooden construction burns away, and when an ice construction melts.
- Every such call goes through the same support and discovery updates as designation work.

## State

- `designations: SortedList<{ tile, kind, priority }>` mirrored in the `designation` grid; the list is the iteration source.
- `engravings: Map<TileIndex, { quality, engraverId, motifId }>`.
- `forbiddenTiles: Set<TileIndex>` (sorted array).
- `cavernBreached: [boolean, boolean, boolean]`.
- `veinsFound: TileIndex[]`: sorted keys of veins already announced.
- `fossilFound: boolean`: the first tile of class `bone` has been dug (absent in older saves and while false).
- `craterTiles: TileIndex[]`: a `starfall` map's crater tiles, ascending, from generation (absent on any other map). 10 reads them for `CraterEntered`.
- `giantSkulls: TileIndex[][]`: each boneyard's skull tiles still to dig, ascending, from generation; a group leaves when its `giant_skull` drops (absent on a map without one).
- `boneyards: TileIndex[][]`: each boneyard's cage tiles, ascending, from generation (absent on a map without one). 10 reads them for `RibsEntered`.
- `boneyardsEntered: boolean[]`: by boneyard, whether a fortress dwarf has stood in its ribs; marked on `RibsEntered` (absent while none has).
- `iceCut: Map<TileIndex, tick>`: each tile of frozen water cut by `harvest_ice` and the tick it was cut, saved as ascending pairs (absent while none waits).
- The fortress record's `igniteConfirmed` (`ConfirmIgnite`; absent: false).
- `layerStack` and `LocalMapSpec` for the legend and for regeneration in tests.

The soil drop's stream `03.soil_drop` is saved in the `rng` section with every other stream; a game that has dug no such soil has none. The saved state retains the changed and discovered tile batches and the levels awaiting relighting until the normal terrain flush. Saving and loading neither dispatch these notifications nor recompute light. An older save without these pending fields starts with empty batches.

## Interfaces

### Reader

- `terrain.designationAt(i)`, `terrain.designations()`
- `terrain.engravingAt(i)`
- `terrain.surfaceZ(x, y)`
- `terrain.isSupported(i)`
- `terrain.cavernBreached(n)`
- `terrain.materialClassAt(i)` (stone, soil, ore, gem, bone, ice, wood, constructed)
- `terrain.spec()` — the `LocalMapSpec`
- `terrain.isForbidden(i)` — the tile's traffic mode is `forbidden`
- `terrain.isCrater(i)` — the tile is one of the `starfall` crater's (`craterTiles`)
- `terrain.boneyardAt(i)` — the boneyard whose ribs hold the tile (its index in `boneyards`), or -1
- `terrain.boneyardEntered(b)` — a fortress dwarf has stood in boneyard `b`'s ribs (`boneyardsEntered`)
- `terrain.forbiddenTiles()` — the forbidden set, ascending (a copy)
- `terrain.inspect(i)` — the look cursor's data for a tile (the inspector's `tile` section)
- `terrain.designationWork(i)` — where a designated tile's work stands: `queued` (its job is open, or it has none yet), `assigned` (a worker holds its job), `blocked` (its job is blocked, or it is a `harvest_ice` designation waiting for its water to freeze), or null with no designation (the map's blink, 23 § Animation)
- `terrain.intendedDrops()` — the items mining owes the world while no item system is present to take them (a copy)

### Terrain API (mutation, called by other systems)

`setKind(i, kind, material)`, `setFlag(i, flag, value)`, `setDesignation(i, kind, priority)`, `clearDesignation(i)`.

### Commands

`Designate { tiles: TileIndex[], kind, priority }`, `UndoDesignation { tiles }`, `SetDesignationPriority { tiles, priority }`, `SetTraffic { tiles, mode }`, `DesignateStairwell { x, y, zTop, zBottom }` (expands to `stairs_updown` on intermediate levels, `stairs_down` at top, `stairs_up` at bottom), `ConfirmDeepsilver` (no payload; sets the fortress's `deepsilverConfirmed` when the player accepts the one-time confirmation), `ConfirmIgnite` (no payload; sets the fortress's `igniteConfirmed` when the player accepts the first fire's confirmation).

### Events consumed

`JobCompleted` (for its own job types), `JobCancelled`, `EntitiesMoved` (discovery), `BuildingPlaced` (clears the stair and channel designations on its footprint, and a ramp designation beneath it), `BuildingCompleted` (support check; the designations worked from a footprint tile the building blocks), `RibsEntered` (marks the boneyard's `boneyardsEntered`).

### Events emitted

`TilesChanged { tiles[] }` (batched), `TilesDiscovered { tiles[] }` (batched), `VeinFound { material, tile }`, `MineralMined { tile, material }`, `FossilFound { dwarfId, tile }`, `DeepsilverMined { tile }`, `CavernBreached { cavern, tile }`, `ConstructionCollapsed { tiles[] }`, `TreeFelled { tile, species }`, `TileIgnited { tile, dwarfId }` (an `ignite` job done).

### Registrations

Job producer `terrain` (designation jobs); overlay `designations`; inspector section `tile` (kind, material, flags, designation, engraving, temperature from 05, fluid from 04). Announcements:
- `VeinFound`: note, string key `announce.vein_found` ("{dwarf} has found {material}.")
- `FossilFound`: note, string key `announce.first_fossil` ("{dwarf} has found the bones of something old.")
- `CavernBreached`: alert, string key `announce.cavern_breached`, which names the cavern and the breach tile ("Cavern 2 is open at (2,14,z13). What lives there can walk in."; an event without them reads "A cavern has been breached.", `announce.cavern_breached_unplaced`); its focus is the breach tile, which the map marks as it marks a log line's focus, and the threats panel's cavern row names it (24 § Announcements and the log)
- `DeepsilverMined`: danger, string key `announce.deepsilver_mined` ("Deepsilver has been mined."), first occurrence only (`once: true`)
- `ConstructionCollapsed`: alert, string key `announce.construction_collapsed` ("A construction has collapsed.")

## Player-facing behavior

- Designation menu (hotkey `d`): dig, channel, stairs (the tool chooses up, down or up/down stairs for each tile, 24 § Input model), ramp, smooth, engrave, remove construction, chop, gather, harvest, traffic, cut ice, set a fire; then a box by two clicks (or two presses of `Enter`) that may span levels, every level between its two corners, or by a drag on one level; with the stairs tool a box over several levels is a stair column on each of its tiles (24 § Input model). A box designates each of its tiles that can take the designation and skips the others. A priority key 1–9 while designating sets the priority; the current priority is shown in the menu. Designations render as overlay glyphs colored by priority. Shift+P or a click on the reprioritize row gives the current priority to the still-designated tiles of the last batch, also kept for Undo; with no batch or no remaining designation, the status row explains why nothing changed.
- Undo: the last designation batch (or a selected rectangle) is cleared if no work has begun; tiles with progress are kept and the UI says how many.
- Announcements: `VeinFound` as a note naming the finder and the material; `FossilFound` as a note naming the finder, once; `CavernBreached` as an alert; `DeepsilverMined` first occurrence as a danger-severity announcement that pauses; `ConstructionCollapsed` as an alert.
- The look cursor shows the material, whether smoothed or engraved, the designation, and "undiscovered" where applicable.

## Extension points

- Multi-tile trees: the `tree` kind and `TreeFelled` already carry species; a canopy would be extra tiles with a `tree_branch` kind and a rule in stage 9.
- Aquifers: a stage between 3 and 4 that marks a layer as `source` water walls (04 already supports source tiles).
- Cave-ins of natural rock: a support rule for natural tiles is the only change.
- New designation kinds: a row in the transition table, a job type in `jobs.md`, and a menu entry.
