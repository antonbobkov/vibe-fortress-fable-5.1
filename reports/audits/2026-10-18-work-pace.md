# Measurement: the work pace

**Task:** T-365 · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Commit examined:** codex-a `e18f855` · **Kind:** measurement; no constant, formula or content value was changed · **Source:** [unmeasured spec numbers](2026-10-17-unmeasured-spec-numbers.md), rank 2

## The answer

- **The job-time arithmetic is exact.** A dig job on granite (hardness 150) takes 90, 69 and 50 ticks at mining skill 0, 4 and 10: `DIG_BASE_TICKS × hardness / (100 + 8 × level)` to the tick; the average over a room is 92 and 51 (a few jobs are paused by a need). Smelting, forging an axe and forging a mail shirt take 150 / 114 / 84, 220 / 167 / 123 and 350 / 266 / 195 ticks at skill 0 / 4 / 10, against 150 / 114 / 83, 220 / 167 / 122 and 350 / 265 / 194 in the formula: the balance model's mining-and-metal table is right to within a tick. `SKILL_TIME_BONUS_PERCENT` = 8 does what the spec says; whether 8 is the right number is a design question the measurement does not answer.
- **What the arithmetic leaves out is most of the time.** A lone miner given a 3×3 granite room needs 3,411 ticks at skill 0 and 2,239 at skill 10 from the designation to the ninth floor, against 810 and 450 of digging: 4.2 and 5.0 times. A ten-tile corridor takes 2,944 and 2,675 ticks against 900 and 500. Of the 2,239 ticks of the skill-10 room, 880 are the walk from wherever the miner was to the next face (the claim-to-start lag is about 210 ticks after each need and 2 ticks between neighbouring tiles) and 900 are needs (sleep 490, drink 249, talk 102, food 61): about 20% digging, 39% walking to work, 40% needs. The balance model's "a ten-tile corridor is a minute's work" holds for the digging only, not for the miner's day.
- **The year-1 fortress of the model is small against what the fixture's dwarves do.** In the economy fixture (seed `three-years`, default priorities, its own wild animals), 112 days: 541 tiles dug and 11 stairs carved (model: about 400), 368 tiles smoothed, 8 workshops (6), 15 bedrooms (10), a dining hall, a hearth hall and a hospital, 352 workshop jobs (23 stone blocks, 41 brews, 49 charcoal, 103 lavish meals, 14 smelts, 9 gems cut, furniture), 23 dwarves at day 112 (13). Wealth 21,143. No tile engraved, and no weapon or armour forged in year 1. The script, not the dwarves, decides what is built, so this shows that the pace is enough for at least this much, not that it is too fast.
- **Mood and wounds cost 0% to 12% of the work.** Mean productivity of the workers at job completion, by job type: 88% for chopping, 98% to 105% for the rest.
- **The marks of the numbers that were measured are replaced by this report:** `DIG_BASE_TICKS`, `SKILL_TIME_BONUS_PERCENT`, `SMOOTH_BASE_TICKS`, `GATHER_BASE_TICKS`, `PLANT_TICKS`, `HARVEST_TICKS`, and `CHOP_BASE_TICKS` with its figure beside it (113 ticks normalised, a tenth over what the formula gives). `BUILD_BASE_TICKS` (every building type overrides it), `CHANNEL_BASE_TICKS`, `ENGRAVE_BASE_TICKS` (not exercised in year 1) and the mood tiers' productivity factors (only their mean effect was seen) stay *unmeasured*.
- **One figure in the balance model was wrong:** "about 100 minutes of a single smith's work" for a 25-tile vein. The recipe times add to about 9,000 ticks at skill 4 (25 smelts 2,850, six suits 5,180, six axes 1,000): 7.5 days, 7.5 minutes of play at 1×. `balance.md` now says so.
- **No number should change on this evidence** (no work file follows): the year-1 fortress digs and builds well over the model (135% of its tiles, 133% of its workshops, 150% of its bedrooms) while its dwarves spend their measured share of time working, so the base times and the skill bonus are not too slow; whether they are too fast needs the later years, which this task did not run.

## What was run

- `reports/data/2026-10-18-work-pace-stepped.probe.ts.txt`: `flat-test` map, the default embark with one miner (every other dwarf's mining labor off), a stair shaft to granite at z 29, a 3×3 room and a 10×1 corridor designated; one miner at skill 0, 4 and 10; the dig jobs from `JobStarted` to `JobCompleted` and from `JobClaimed` to `JobStarted`, and the miner's behaviour each tick. Results: `…-mining.json`. Then a smelter and a forge on a bare map, one dwarf at skill 0, 4 and 10 with the labor on, inputs on the floor beside the workshop; `smelt_hematite`, `forge_battle_axe` and `forge_mail_shirt` ordered once: the craft job from `JobStarted` to `JobCompleted`, and the order to the product (90 to 100 ticks more). Results: `…-workshop.json`.
- `reports/data/2026-10-18-work-pace-year-one.probe.ts.txt`: the three-year fixture (`threeYearsWorld('three-years')`, `setupThreeYearsFortress`, `playThreeYears` with the scenario's checks every 100 then 300 ticks) for 112 days, every `JobStarted` and `JobCompleted` with the worker's skill and productivity, every `ItemCrafted`; each job's duration normalised to skill 0 (`duration × (100 + 8 × level) / 100`). Results: `…-year-one.json` (medians, 10th and 90th percentiles, mean productivity, the highest skill by fortnight, the room counts). One run; the fixture is deterministic.

Normalised medians (base in brackets): haul 5 (5), dig 57 (60 × hardness/100, mixed soil and rock), gather 40 (40), plant 41 (40), harvest 41 (40), smooth 115 (120), chop 113 (90), carve stairs 56 (72 × hardness/100), crafts by recipe (make_barrel 72, make_bed 108, smelt_hematite 139 of 150). A job's duration includes the pauses of needs that interrupted it, so these are upper bounds; the stepped runs have the exact times.

## What this does not measure

- Years 2 and 3, whether the work runs out, and any engraving.
- A fortress with a player's priorities; the fixture is a script with fixed orders.
- The mood factor by tier. Only the mean productivity at job completion is seen.
- Other job types: butchering, hunting, fishing, hauling to a depot; and the dwarves' walking pace, about 0.09 tiles a tick in [the no-drink diagnosis](2026-10-18-no-drink-deaths-diagnosis.md) against 0.15 in `balance.md`.
