## How long crowds last

`CrowdWatch` looks every 20 ticks over the three years of all 20 seeds. A tile is crowded here when it slows anyone, so holds more than `CROWD_FREE` standing creatures. The count stops at the cap, so a crowd of 6 means 6 or more.

| Place | Looks | Crowded | At the cap | Worst | Longest on one tile |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Door tiles | 747,709 | 1,749 (0.2%) | 3 | 6 | 120 ticks |
| Hearth-hall tiles | 10,997,700 | 429,960 (3.9%) | 323,460 | 6 | 379,180 ticks, the whole run |
| Squad station tiles | 24,831 | 7,443 (30%) | 14 | 6 | 1,100 ticks |
| Tiles of soldiers with a target | 2,047 | 56 (2.7%) | 0 | 5 | 260 ticks |

- **The hall** is nearly all one tile: about 1 in 25 of its tiles, crowded about 90% of the time. A probe of `three-years-p0-2` on days 40 to 120 found only that tile crowded at each look, and only dogs on it, four rising to eleven, with no path ([task](../../docs/work/T-463-dogs-on-one-hall-tile.md)).
- **Stations:** a unit test shows six archers on one station hold six tiles and keep their base cooldown, against twice it when piled on one tile. The 30% is the point itself in the three-year script, which stations its squad on the gate's stair or the foot of it, the way between the surface and the halls. Passers-by there are the likely cause; who stands there was not journalled. Only the member posted on that tile is affected.
- **Soldiers fighting** are rarely crowded. Closing on a target is not spread, so a large squad in the open could still pile up; this file shows it does not much.
