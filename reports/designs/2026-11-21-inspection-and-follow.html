Inspecting things that move

Today the Look panel shows a tile. This proposal makes it show a thing: a dwarf, an animal, an item. The panel stays with that thing while it walks or is carried, the view can follow it, and a click on the map opens the most interesting thing there with no tab to press.

  • A click opens the best thingCreature, then item, then building, then stockpile or zone, then the bare tile.
  • A crowd gives a listSeveral creatures or items on one tile open a numbered list, dwarves first.
  • The view belongs to the thingIt says where the thing is now and stays open while it moves.
  • Centre and follow[S-c] jumps to it. [S-f] keeps it in view until you move the view.

1. How inspection works today

Look is a tile panel. The fortress screen keeps a map cursor, lookTile() returns the cursor's tile, and drawLookPanel redraws whatever stands on that tile each frame (src/ui/panels/look.ts, src/ui/screens/fortress.ts).

  • It has three tabs: [1] Tile, [2] Items, [3] Who. A click opens the Tile tab, where each creature and item is one line. A dwarf's details need 3.
  • The Who tab draws every creature on the tile one under another in a single scroll.
  • Inspecting a dwarf from Units, Mood or a name in the log (inspectCreature) centres the map once and opens the same tile panel with that dwarf first.
  • A click on a building opens the building's own panel. A stockpile's settings are reached only through Zones: z, then a click.
  • Nothing follows anything. [S-c] center exists in the Units panel alone, as a single jump.

Three things go wrong with this:

  • The click seldom lands on what was meant. The question is usually "who is this?", and the answer is a key press and a scroll away.
  • The panel loses its subject. A dwarf worth watching is usually one that is going somewhere.
  • Watching takes constant panning. The one centring key is in another panel and jumps once.

2. What a click opens

A left click on the map with no tool armed walks down this ladder and opens the first thing it finds. Enter at the map cursor does the same, so the keyboard has the same way in.

  • A loose item lies on the tile or in a stockpile there. A barrel counts as one item and shows what it holds inside its own view. Goods a building holds (a workshop's stock, the wagon's load) belong to the building and do not count at step 2; they are listed under the building in the tile list.
  • Only what the map draws counts. The compositor leaves out a stealthed hostile no dwarf has spotted. creaturesAt in look.ts has no such test, so the ladder must apply the map's rule, and it is worth checking whether Look gives such a creature away today.
  • A right click with no tool always opens the tile list, whatever is there. So does a second left click on a tile whose one creature or item is already open. Everything on a tile is two clicks away at most.
  • With a tool armed or a panel picking (a station, a link, a zone to select) a click keeps the meaning it has today.

3. The three views

The tabs go. Look becomes three views that lead into each other: the tile list, the creature view and the item view. The title row names what is shown.

Tile list

The view of a place. It opens for a crowd, for bare ground, for a right click, and while the keyboard cursor roams.

Creature view

The view of one dwarf, animal or hostile. It holds the creature's id, never a tile.

  • Pinned on top: the identity line, the activity, and an At (x, y, z) line that is read fresh every frame. When others share the tile it adds "2 more here". On another level it adds "1 level down".
  • In the middle: the sections Look draws now, in the same order (needs, skills, attributes, mood, health, equipment, relations). They scroll under the header.
  • Pinned at the foot: the keys. [S-c] center, [S-f] follow, [h] here for the tile list of where it stands now, [Bksp] back to the view before.
  • The map cursor sits on the creature and moves with it, so the eye can find it again.
  • Names are links. A carried or worn item opens its item view, a friend opens that dwarf. Bksp returns.

Item view

The same idea for a stack of items. It holds the item's id, and its place is whatever items.tileOf answers, which already sees through a carrier or a container.

4. Centre and follow

  • [S-c] center is one jump: the view centres on the thing and goes to its level. It is the key Units already uses.
  • [S-f] follow is a switch. Turning it on centres once, then keeps the thing in view. The footer shows ■ on and status row 2 reads "Following Tova Forgemantle · move the view to stop".
  • Following does not pin the thing to the centre. The view moves only as far as it must to keep the thing inside the middle half of the map view, and changes level when the thing does. A dwarf pottering in a workshop leaves the map still; one that sets off across the fortress drags it along a tile at a time.
  • Moving the view ends it: the arrows, a middle drag, Space and drag, Shift and the wheel, edge scrolling, a level key or the wheel, Home, a click on a place in the log or the job list, and the jump a danger line makes. So do closing the panel, opening another thing, and the thing being gone.
  • These leave it on: zoom, pause and speed, scrolling the panel.

The other way to follow is to keep the thing dead centre. On a glyph grid that slides every tile of the map on every step, which makes the map hard to read while the one thing you follow stands still on screen. The box moves the map less and still never loses the subject.

5. When the thing is gone

A view of a thing can outlive the thing. It then keeps its title, says what happened, gives the last place, and ends follow. It never falls back silently to a tile.

None of these collide. h is one of the letters 24 § Input model keeps free. S-c is Units' centre key. S-f is Traffic in Designate and Forbid for a dragged selection, both other contexts. The digits take over from the three tab keys. Each is a row of the keymap, so Settings can rebind it. Every key is also a button in the footer, so the mouse reaches all of it.

8. Building it

  • State. lookTab and lookCreature on the fortress screen give way to one selection: a tile, a creature id or an item id, with a follow flag and a short back history. It is presentation state like the cursor and is not saved.
  • The ladder is a pure function of the readers and a tile in look.ts, next to creaturesAt and itemsAt, so unit tests cover every rung.
  • Where a thing is. A creature's Position; an item's items.tileOf(id). Each frame the cursor is set from it, and with follow on the camera is nudged through the existing pan and centerOn.
  • Ending follow. The pan, level, Home and focus handlers in fortress.ts clear the flag; they are few and already in one file.
  • Drawing. drawLookPanel splits into a tile, a creature and an item drawer. The creature sections in look.ts and look-dwarf.ts are reused as they are; the mock-ups above were made by calling them.
  • The zone step opens Zones with the zone selected. Zones opened this way must leave map clicks to the ladder; Zones opened with z keeps selecting zones.
  • The Tile tab's buttons (pull lever, open, deconstruct) move to the building row and its panel. Check that the building panel offers the lever pull before the tab goes.
  • Documents and tests. 24 § Inspection and § Input model, the Inspection panel wireframe in fortress-screen.md, the keymap table and its parity scenario, the look strings, and the browser specs that press 1 2 3 in Look.

It cuts into three tasks that each leave the game whole: the selection, the ladder, the tile list and the creature view; then the item view with links and back; then centre, follow and the gone states.

9. Choices made here

You gave the order dwarf, item, stockpile, tile. These are the gaps I filled. Each is cheap to turn the other way before work starts.

  • Buildings sit between items and zonesYour list did not name them. Today a click on a workshop opens its panel, and that should survive on every workshop tile that has no dwarf or loose item on it. The cost: a click on the very tile where a dwarf works opens the dwarf, and the workshop is its neighbour tile or a row in the list.
  • Any zone, not stockpiles aloneA bedroom, pasture or hospital tile with nothing on it opens that zone's inspector. The alternative is stockpiles only, with other zones as a row of the tile view.
  • Creatures means all creaturesDwarves come first in a list, then animals, then the rest, but a lone dog or raider opens its own view as a dwarf does.
  • One list, creatures firstYou asked for a list of dwarves before a list of items. This is one list with the dwarves on top and the items under them, so a mixed tile needs no second step.
  • The follow box, not dead centreSection 4 gives the reason. Dead centre is a one-line change if you prefer it once you see it move.
  • Opening another panel drops the selectionThe sidebar shows one panel, and keeping a hidden selection that still steers the camera would surprise. Follow ends with it.
  • No item commands yetForbid, dump and send to the depot would sit well in the item view's footer. They are left for a later task to keep this one about looking.
