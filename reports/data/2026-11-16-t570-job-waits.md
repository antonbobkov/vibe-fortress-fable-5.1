# T-570: how long the fixtures' routine jobs wait, and the stone at the depot

The probe `test/perf/t570_job_waits_probe.test.ts` (`gh workflow run measure.yml -f suite=job_waits`) plays a three-year seed at a shift, or `defence_year` (`defence`), and looks at the job board every 100 ticks. A job found `open` (neither claimed nor blocked) starts an open spell, which ends at the first look that finds it claimed, blocked or gone. Each cell is a workshop's or a building's mean spell / longest spell, in days; `-` where the run has no such job. The last column is the day the most raw stone lay at the depot, with the stone owned, available and at the depot that day.

What the long waits were, from the probe's notes: every one was a job at priority 5 (or a defence work at 4) that nobody claimed while the dwarves with its labor took the food work a step ahead or the smoothing, digging and hauling of the same priority nearer to hand. None was blocked. The kitchen's waits are short: its labor is in the food step. In `defence_year` the forge's job at the squad's kit priority stood open from day 35 to the end, its named worker, the smith, at drill in the squad (left to [T-NEW](../../docs/work/T-NEW-defence-smith-at-drill.md)).

The raw stone held at the depot was sent there by the script's `trade` for a caravan (stone above what the mason's orders want, while the anvil is to be paid for or a visit's cargo is thin) and stood there until a caravan took it, or for good: in `defence_year` 15 stones from day 77 to the end while the stone available fell to none by day 217. Goods at the depot are kept from every other job (20), as they must be, and nothing in the script took them back.
