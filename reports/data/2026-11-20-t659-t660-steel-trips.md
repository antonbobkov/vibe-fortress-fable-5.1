# T-659 and T-660: the steel's trips and the smith's refetched steel

**Tasks:** T-659, T-660 · **Date:** 2026-11-20 · **Code:** branch `t659-steel-trips` on master `cfe4afee` (T-652, T-655, T-657, T-658); the probes ran on `8d23573e` (T-659's merge alone) and `d9a98d7e` (both tasks); the sweep ran on `4e99d2c1`, with master's T-656 merged in · **Source:** [the T-659 work file](../../docs/archive/tasks/T-659-steel-fuel-in-one-trip.md), [the T-660 work file](../../docs/archive/tasks/T-660-smith-steel-refetched.md), [T-655's measurement](2026-11-20-t655-furnace-jobs-fetch.md)

## How

`test/perf/t660_smith_steel_probe.test.ts` plays a three-year seed at a sweep shift on the sweep's cadence and, from day 126 to day 336, follows every furnace job: its claims, its reservations (`ItemReserved`, which T-655 counted as trips), its pickups (the worker taking an item in hand for it: the trips walked), and every interruption of a steel job or of a job at the second smelter: the event 11 was handling when it came (a critical need and which, a threat, an alert) or the board's own call (the hold of an absent worker), the job's state, the item in hand, what was delivered and still to fetch, each lapse of its reservations, and the next job its worker claimed and after how long. Every 20 ticks it samples each furnace worker (working, walking empty, carrying at its own workshop's job) and, while a furnace's job stands open, what that workshop's named worker is doing.

```
T660_PROBE=1 T660_SEED=three-years-p4-6 T660_PERIL=4 T660_SHIFT=50 T660_DAYS=336 T660_FROM=126 npx vitest run -c vitest.perf.config.ts test/perf/t660_smith_steel_probe.test.ts
T660_PROBE=1 T660_SEED=three-years-p2-1 T660_PERIL=2 T660_SHIFT=0 T660_DAYS=336 T660_FROM=126 npx vitest run -c vitest.perf.config.ts test/perf/t660_smith_steel_probe.test.ts
```

About 15 minutes a run locally; one timeline each, and the timelines part as soon as the code acts differently, so the jobs done move with them. The sweep below is the measure.

## T-660: why the smith's steel was fetched again

**T-655's "8 to 13 trips" were reservations, not walks.** A steel job at the second smelter reserved 8.3 (`p4-6`) and 9.1 (`p2-1`) items, but its worker took 5.0 and 5.5 items in hand, against the recipe's four. Most reservations were the same items reserved again after their hold lapsed: an interrupted job keeps its undelivered reservations for `RESERVE_KEEP_TICKS` (600), the smith came back to it 920 to 1150 ticks later on average, and by then they had lapsed (51 of 72 interruptions in `p2-1`). A lapse costs no walk: the claim takes the nearest items again.

**The interruptions were the smith's own rest.** In `p2-1` (the build with T-659's merge alone; master's showed the same in its stack) 65 of the 72 interruptions of the second smelter's steel were `NeedCritical` for rest, 6 the hold of a worker away at a need, 1 a threat; none was the forge, an alert or a patient. A steel job takes long to fetch: four inputs, the flux stone heavy, from 6 to 14 steps. Rest rises from its want mark (60) to its critical mark (90) in 300 ticks, and a worker at a job does not stop for a want (10 § Behavior selection, step 6 is at the choice of a job), so a job that spans that window is interrupted at the critical mark wherever the worker is. The first smelter's worker, at nothing but the smelter, was interrupted the same way, 1.9 times a steel job against the smith's 2.7.

**Half of the interruptions came with an input in hand, and that is the refetch.** The worker dropped it where it stood (36 of 72 in `p2-1`; 8 of 18 in `p4-6`), most often the flux stone on its way from the far stack, and fetched it again from there after its sleep: the trips over four.

**The forge is not what holds the smith.** After an interruption the smith claimed the same steel job next 57 times of 72 (`p2-1`; 13 of 18 in `p4-6`), the forge's job 12 times. While the second smelter's steel stood open (74% of the samples in `p2-1`), the smith was asleep 37% of that time, eating, drinking or sitting 40%, at the forge 13%, sheltering 4%. The first smelter's worker, alone at his workshop, shows the same shares (asleep 42%). The second smelter is no worse off for its worker's two workshops; both smelters wait on one worker's day.

## T-660: what changed

**Tried and left: a want served between two trips.** A worker between two trips of a fetch, nothing in hand, with hunger, thirst or rest at its want mark served it then, holding the job. It cut the second smelter's interruptions (2.7 to 1.7 a steel job in `p2-1`, 2.4 to 1.4 in `p4-6`), the claims (3.7 to 2.7) and the trips (5.5 to 3.6), but the furnace workers then served every want as it came rather than at the critical mark, and spent more of the day on errands: the steel jobs done fell from 67 to 35 in `p2-1` and from 21 to 12 in `p4-6`, and `defence_year`'s squad ended empty in CI. Reverted.

**The rule (11 § Job steps, interruptions): an input in hand is delivered first.** A critical need that comes while a worker carries an undelivered input of a job at a building (a workshop's craft, a construction) lets it carry that input to the workshop or the site; the job is interrupted on the worker's first step with nothing in hand. A threat, an alert, a call to a patient or a worker going down interrupts at once as before, and a haul still drops its item. The rest the worker carries on with is a few dozen ticks at most. `test/unit/jobs/t660-deliver-before-need.test.ts`.

The script is unchanged: the smith keeps the second smelter. A worker of its own cost the sweep's living in T-642, and the probe finds the smith's two workshops are not what interrupts the steel.

## T-659: the steel's two fuels

**Decided: one input of two units.** A recipe that takes fuel into its product (steel's carbon) asks for the bar its job burns together with it, one input of two bars (12 § Fuel; 11 § Assignment step 4): a steel job has three inputs, and a stack of two comes in one trip. A half fuel is never steel's carbon and stays an input of its own; on a workshop magma-powered by the time the job completes, only the carbon is consumed. `test/unit/workshops/t659-steel-fuel-one-trip.test.ts`.

**And the nearest stacks when they walk less.** With the merge alone, T-655's rule (the fewest stacks, then the nearest of those) sent the first smelter's worker in `p4-6` across the hall for a stack of two charcoal while single bars of coal lay beside the smelter: the fuel trips went from 3 to 11 steps (1.5 bars a trip). A unit input now takes the nearest stacks in search order, more trips, when their summed estimated distance from the job is smaller than the fewest stacks' (11 § Assignment step 4, `algorithms/jobs-and-hauling.md`): two single bars beside the smelter before a stack of two across the hall, and two near stacks of two before a far stack of four, which reverses one of T-655's cases (its unit test now expects the near pair; a new case keeps the single stack when the nearest make-up walks farther).

**Flux stone by the smelter: not as a player can.** The flux came 6 to 18 steps (10 to 13 on most timelines), about as far as the iron bars and farther than the fuel, from where it was mined. A stockpile filters by category and material class (13 § Stockpiles), and flux stone is the class `stone` with every other rock, so a stone stockpile by the smelter would draw the whole mine's stone into the workshop hall. The script leaves the flux where it lies; a follow-up asks for a stockpile that takes flux alone ([T-NEW](../../docs/archive/tasks/T-665-flux-stockpile.md)).
